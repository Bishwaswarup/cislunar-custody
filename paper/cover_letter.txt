Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Custody Horizons for Ground-Based Angles-Only Tracking of
Cislunar Periodic Orbits", for publication in The Journal of the Astronautical Sciences as a research
article.

Ground-based optical telescopes are the most accessible sensors for cislunar space domain awareness,
but they lose sight of their targets for days at a time. The manuscript asks a question that
operators face after every such gap: how long can an object on a cislunar periodic orbit go
unobserved before a telescope search can no longer find it, and what determines that time? We
define this custody horizon for a search centred on the operator's own unscented prediction and map
it over a year of physically generated observations from a four-site network, for four
representative orbits and for 30 members of five orbit families. The main findings are that the
near-stable NRHO and DRO keep custody beyond 30 days while unstable L1 and L2 orbits are lost after
11-17 days, mainly depending on the length of the preceding tracking arc; that the horizon is
predicted by the unscented transform of the post-fit covariance but not by the finite-time Lyapunov
exponent; that a Gaussian-mixture filter reacquires reliably after a 3-day gap where the extended
Kalman filter fails; and that realistic site biases and solar-pressure errors barely shorten the
horizons but make covariance-based custody claims overconfident. The conclusions are checked in a
DE440 ephemeris model.

The paper builds on cislunar orbit determination and custody studies published in this journal and
should interest readers working on space domain awareness, sensor tasking and orbit determination.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by any
other journal. No preprint has been posted. The author has no competing interests. The code, the
data and a script that checks every number in the manuscript against those data are openly
available (see the Data and code availability statement). The use of an AI assistant is declared in
the Methods section, as the journal requires.
